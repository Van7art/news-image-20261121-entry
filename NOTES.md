## Variants

All three were drawn programmatically with PIL (DejaVu fonts, no
AI-generated imagery, so no garbled text). Each is 1024×1024 PNG.

### V1 — Newspaper front page ("THE WORLD") — WINNER
Masthead + date banner, lead story (Poland missiles, with deck and a
missile panel giving missile type / no injuries / Rutte's visit /
Poland's NATO ask), a numbered "Day at a glance" column (stories
2–5), and an "Also today" strip (stories 6–8) with a sources footer.
- Strengths: clearest hierarchy; the date is the biggest element
  after the masthead; all 8 stories have a headline plus a deck;
  the lead story gets a full paragraph; at 300 px wide the masthead,
  date, lead headline and all eight headlines stay readable.
- Weaknesses: dense; the missile icon is abstract (a streaking
  missile, not a photograph — but no photographs are available to
  this build, and a drawn icon cannot be mistaken for a real one).

### V2 — Magazine cover ("SATURDAY")
Big "SATURDAY" masthead, date in a gold band, a large streaking
missile as the lead visual, one headline banner, four cover lines
plus an "ALSO" strip.
- Strengths: boldest single image; the missile visual is the most
  striking of the three; very clean at 300 px.
- Weaknesses: only 4 stories get a headline + deck; the other three
  are squeezed into one small "ALSO" line, so breadth (judge
  criterion 2) is weaker; the big empty night-sky area wastes space
  that could carry news.

### V3 — Editorial ledger ("THE WORLD IN NUMBERS")
Numbered rows for all 8 stories with a tag, headline and one-line
deck, plus a "By the numbers" strip (-3.7% S&P, -5.3% Nasdaq, 400+
drones, 3 missiles, 370,000 evacuated, $59.7bn NSE).
- Strengths: all 8 stories fit cleanly and scan fast; the numbers
  strip is memorable; the cleanest layout of the three.
- Weaknesses: flat and ledger-like — less of a "picture of the day"
  than a front page; one-line decks carry less of each story than
  V1 (no lead paragraph, no panel detail); the numbers strip repeats
  facts already in the rows.

## Choice

**V1 wins.** Against the judge's criteria, in order: (1) it names
the day biggest and ties every story to it, including a genuinely
21-November element in the lead panel (Rutte due in Warsaw today);
(2) it has the widest breadth — 8 stories across conflict, markets,
science, climate, security, weather, sport and business, with a
headline and a deck each; (3) it reads best — a viewer who missed
the day can tell what happened from the image alone, and it stays
legible at ~300 px; (4) every fact is as reported, with sources in
the footer, the Riemann item worded as a claim, and the 2024 Baku
pact correctly left out; (5) one clear front-page composition,
cleanly drawn, no overlaps or clipped text (fixed after a first
visual check: panel text and the "Also today" decks were shortened).
V2 is the prettier single image but carries less news; V3 is the
neatest list but the flattest picture.
